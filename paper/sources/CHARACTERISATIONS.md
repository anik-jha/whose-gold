# Characterisation attestations

One line per cited work this paper describes as showing or finding something.
Format: `` - `key` -- <our sentence> || <quoted text from the source> ``

A checker can confirm a reference resolves to a real record. It cannot confirm the sentence
beside it is fair. This file records that the source was opened and where the support sits.
Provenance is named per quote, because "the abstract states it plainly" and "I read the
repository's reported metrics" are different strengths of evidence.

- `zhang2025diverging` -- They study disagreement in MultiPref and HelpSteer2 directly, build a taxonomy of its causes, show that most disagreement is not annotation error, and release MultiPref-Disagreements. || Abstract: "We develop a taxonomy of disagreement sources spanning ten categories across four high-level classes and find that the majority of disagreements are due to factors such as task underspecification or response style. Our findings challenge a standard assumption in reward modeling methods that annotator disagreements can be attributed to simple noise." The dataset names are not in the abstract and were confirmed from the paper's own description of its release: it introduces "MultiPref-Disagreements and HelpSteer2-Disagreements by releasing the individual annotations of these existing datasets". Every clause of our sentence is theirs, including the artifact name.
- `zhang2025diverging` -- Their unit is the annotator pair, taken over all permutations of the annotators on an item; there is no leaderboard or Kendall tau analysis in their paper, whose win/loss/tie rates compare response types under an LLM judge. || Checked 2026-10-02 against https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14632v3. Figure 1 caption: "We used all permutations of annotator and response pairs and remove repeated values." Table 5 caption: "LLM-as-Judge Results over Comply vs. Refuse (row 1) and Refuse vs. Refuse (rows 2 to 5) diverging preferences that differ in various attributes", with columns Win, Loss, Tie. The strings "Kendall" and "leaderboard" do not occur in that text; "rank" occurs only where they "rank each example in the WildBench Benchmark by the divisiveness of the prompt". This replaces the accepted paper's claim of no win-rate analysis, which Table 5 contradicts.
- `zhang2025diverging` -- HelpSteer2 keeps several annotators' ratings per response, from which Zhang et al. derive preferences. || Same source, Section 2: "each preference pair is annotated by 3-5 different annotators. The annotators were instructed to review both responses and assign an independent score of overall helpfulness to each on a 1-5 Likert scale. To identify annotator preferences, we take the difference between the overall scores assigned to each response". They cite HelpSteer2 (arXiv:2406.08673) for this data, not HelpSteer2-Preference, which replaces the accepted paper's "exploit exactly that".
- `miranda2024hybrid` -- MultiPref's Limitations states, for its routing model, an assumption of no intra-group annotator variability. Both pools are Prolific crowdworkers who passed the same qualification test; the expert designation requires a graduate degree where the normal pool requires a bachelor's. || Checked 2026-10-02 against https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19133 (v5). Limitations, "Intra-group variability of annotators": "One of our key assumptions is that there is no variability in intra-group annotators for both humans and LMs. When HyPER decides to route a preference instance to a human or an LM, we don't make fine-grained decisions as to what type of human annotator (or which LM) should annotate. However, we believe that MultiPref can enable this type of analyses especially for direct human feedback, as the dataset disambiguates between normal and expert crowdworker annotations. We leave this exploration for future work." Our quotation elides the middle with an ellipsis. Appendix C, Human annotation: "We recruit normal crowdworkers from Prolific with at least 99% approval rate, fluent in English, and have completed a Bachelor's degree. Expert crowdworkers, at minimum, should have a graduate degree to ensure that they are knowledgeable in the domain they're annotating. Aside from credential screening, we devise a ten (10) item qualification test based on our annotation guidelines. Participants must score at least 90% to be included in the study". Appendix C also routes prompts to experts by domain: "In order to route annotation instances to relevant domain experts, we first classify each prompt to eleven (11) highest-level academic degrees based on Prolific's categorization."
- `kirk2024prism` -- PRISM assigns one participant per conversation; different participants rate an identical prompt-response pair only where one recurs. || Checked 2026-10-02 against https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16019. Data description: "Each row is a single conversation, identified by a conversation_id, that can be matched back to a participant's survey profile via the user_id." Appendix S.5, "Exact Prompt-Response Pairs with Multiple Ratings": "Now we consider regions of Prism where different participants rate the exact same prompt-response pairs." This replaces the accepted paper's "no two annotators ever rate the same item".
- `wang2024helpsteer2pref` -- HelpSteer2-Preference adds pairwise preferences from three to five annotators per item, contracted through one vendor. || Checked 2026-10-02 against https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01257: "The vendor (Scale AI) was asked to distribute each task to 3-5 annotators to independently label preference among two responses for every prompt." and "Annotators who supported the construction of the dataset were contracted through Scale AI".
- `stableval2026` -- The nearest antecedent on the aggregate side: it models annotator confusion and reports that majority vote yields ranking instability under annotator heterogeneity. || Abstract of arXiv:2605.02122: "annotator disagreement, bias, and variability make system rankings fragile under standard majority vote aggregation" and "Across controlled synthetic experiments and multiple real-world human-annotated benchmarks, majority vote exhibits increasing score error and ranking instability under annotator heterogeneity and adversarial noise". Because its evidence includes real human-annotated benchmarks, the accepted paper's contrast that "their heterogeneity is constructed" was unsupported and was removed on 2026-10-02; the camera-ready contrasts only our two recruited populations and six-model boards with their general statement.
- `perspectivist2026` -- Perspectivist evaluation argues that annotator disagreement is signal rather than noise. || Abstract: "While early approaches treated disagreement as noise to be removed, recent work increasingly models it as a meaningful signal reflecting variation in interpretation and perspective." Near-verbatim; it is a survey, and we cite it as characterising a line rather than as establishing a result.
- `positionbias2026` -- Our judges' order-swap reversal rates are consistent with published position-bias measurements. || Not on arXiv, so not machine-checkable; read from the benchmark repository itself (github.com/lechmazur/position_bias), which measures whether models keep their preference when "two lightly edited versions of the same story are shown in opposite orders" and reports a median order-flip rate of 41.3% and a model-average first-shown pick rate of 64.3%. Our 44.6 / 24.5 / 23.7% straddle that median, so "consistent with" is the right strength. **It reports no verbosity or output-format results**, which is why the Related work sentence was narrowed on 2026-08-22 to claim position effects only.
- `judgesense2026` -- Finds judge verdicts unstable under semantically equivalent prompt rephrasings, a family of sensitivity it reports as largely unexamined. || Abstract: "the stability of their verdicts under semantically equivalent prompt rephrasings remains largely unexamined" and "Pairwise evaluation tasks consistently exhibit position bias." The words verbosity, format, output and JSON do not appear in it. Our output-format finding is our own measurement and is no longer described as replicating this work.
- `jha2026decorrelation` -- In a different setting, 24 open-weight monitors scoring backdoored code, a monitor's agreement with the rest of its pool tracks its own detection skill (Spearman 0.84) rather than its pretraining lineage (+0.064, p = 0.18). || Abstract of arXiv:2608.16190: "We study 24 open-weight monitors spanning nine pretraining lineages ... on backdoored code", "a monitor's own skill predicts its agreement with the pool (Spearman 0.84, n = 24, permutation p below 0.0001)" and "lineage barely moves the metric either (+0.064, p = 0.18)". The pool is the pool of monitors, not a human annotator pool; the accepted paper called the monitors judges and left the setting implicit, which the camera-ready corrects on 2026-10-02. Cited only as an analogy for why unlike systems need not have independent errors.
- `kadekodi2025selective` -- They abstain from comparisons where fewer than a chosen fraction of individuals agree and return a partial order rather than arbitrating dissent. || Abstract (ICML 2025, PMLR v267 pp. 28644-28669): "we introduce a paradigm for selective aggregation, where we can avoid the need to arbitrate dissent by abstaining from comparison. We summarize collective preferences as a selective ranking - i.e., a partial order where we can only compare items where at least 100*(1 - tau)% of individuals agree." Not on arXiv, so not machine-checkable; author list, title, volume and pages read from the PMLR proceedings page. Cited as the other route open to an aggregator, not as a competing measurement.
